# Adversarial content review: Modules 01–05

Reviewed 2026-09-14. Scope: Windows & Acquisition, Left Ventricular Function, Right Ventricular Function, Pericardium, and IVC & Volume Status. These are the five display modules preceding lung ultrasound. This report complements the separate lung review; it does not revise teaching content.

The most consequential problems are in Modules 2 and 3: findings such as low EF, low TAPSE, and McConnell's sign become diagnoses or treatment instructions without enough clinical qualification. Module 1 also contains concrete orientation and landmark errors. Modules 4 and 5 handle uncertainty substantially better; this review established no high-priority defect in either.

## High-priority findings

### M2-01 — High: Low EF becomes proof of cardiogenic shock and an automatic inotrope indication

**Locations:** [overview](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/02-lv.qmd:17), [severe dysfunction](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/02-lv.qmd:123), [Case 2 answer](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/02-lv.qmd:256).

The overview and severe-dysfunction section equate reduced EF plus hypotension with cardiogenic shock. Case 2 then diagnoses a cardiogenic component and instructs the learner to add an inotrope. The supplied BP, 72/40, corresponds to an approximate MAP of 51 mm Hg. The answer does say to avoid vasopressor escalation **alone**; it does not tell learners to stop vasopressors. Nevertheless, its automatic inotrope instruction and explanation about vasoconstricting a failing heart underplay the unresolved perfusion-pressure problem.

EF is not cardiac output. A primary septic-shock study documented depressed EF despite normal or elevated cardiac index. Current SSC guidance conditions inotrope use on persistent hypoperfusion despite adequate volume and arterial pressure, and retains required vasopressors. [Parker et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6703540/), [SSC 2026 adult recommendations](https://www.sccm.org/survivingsepsiscampaign/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-adult-guidelines).

**Correction:** Teach possible mixed shock; assess forward flow, perfusion, congestion, and fluid responsiveness. Continue necessary pressure support and consider an inotrope for the appropriate physiological indication. Reassess the immediate response to treatment; a later repeat echo should not imply waiting hours to judge resuscitation.

**Counterexample:** A patient with chronic EF 25% develops septic vasoplegia while maintaining adequate output. Low EF alone does not establish that an inodilator is the missing intervention. Module 5 already teaches this distinction at lines 139 and 178.

### M2-02 — High: RV infarction is an unconditional instruction to give volume

**Locations:** [inferior-wall section](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/02-lv.qmd:178), [quiz answer](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/02-lv.qmd:323).

Both passages say RV infarction requires volume loading. That omits current filling pressure, congestion, concomitant LV injury, and response to a trial. In a small randomized crossover study of severe RV infarction with elevated RA pressure, volume increased filling pressures without improving cardiac index. This directly defeats the unconditional rule; it does not establish that fluids never help. [Primary hemodynamic study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0002914994903980).

**Correction:** Assess preload and congestion. In an underfilled, hypotensive patient, consider a cautious fluid challenge with immediate reassessment; stop if ineffective or congestion worsens. Preserve urgent reperfusion and appropriate vasoactive support.

**Counterexample:** RV infarction with a distended RV, high RA pressure, septal shift, and pulmonary edema may deteriorate with additional fluid. Synchronize the narrative and quiz.

### M2-03 — High: The myocarditis case excludes ischemia prematurely and makes MRI the definitive endpoint

**Location:** [Case 4 answer](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/02-lv.qmd:294).

Young age, viral prodrome, global hypokinesis, and a small effusion support suspected myocarditis but do not prove its cause or exclude coronary disease. Calling MRI the gold standard also obscures the role of endomyocardial biopsy in high-risk or hemodynamically unstable myocarditis when histology can change urgent therapy. [ESC 2025 myocarditis/pericarditis guideline](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/46/40/3952/8234483).

**Correction:** Use “suspected fulminant myocarditis with shock,” involve cardiology/the shock team urgently, evaluate alternatives including ACS, and explain that CMR and biopsy have different roles according to stability and clinical indications. Retain the existing instruction not to delay resuscitation.

**Counterexample:** Giant-cell myocarditis may require biopsy-directed treatment. Conversely, global dysfunction on a limited examination cannot reliably exclude diffuse ischemic injury.

### M3-01 — High: TAPSE becomes both a diagnosis and an urgency score

**Locations:** [quiz Q5 answer](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/03-rv.qmd:282), with related interpretation at [line 143](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/03-rv.qmd:143).

The quiz says a newly reduced TAPSE of 10 mm establishes acute severe RV failure with immediate collapse risk, then contrasts chronic dysfunction as less immediately dangerous. TAPSE measures one component of RV motion and requires integration with other findings. Decompensated chronic pulmonary hypertension can itself be an emergency. [ASE 2025 right-heart guideline](https://www.asecho.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/PIIS0894731725000379.pdf), [ESC/ERS pulmonary hypertension guideline, section 6.3.7](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erj/61/1/2200879).

**Correction:** A new reduction warrants prompt review of acquisition, loading conditions, other RV findings, and perfusion. Assign urgency from the clinical syndrome and explicitly include acute-on-chronic RV failure.

**Counterexamples:** Reduced annular excursion after cardiac surgery need not signify circulatory collapse. A patient with longstanding PH who develops hypotension, oliguria, and rising lactate may need immediate escalation.

### M3-02 — High: McConnell's sign receives overly general specificity and a treatment-facing shortcut

**Locations:** [sign description](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/03-rv.qmd:133), [quiz answer](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/03-rv.qmd:268).

The text presents approximately 94% specificity without explaining the comparator population, then connects the sign with consideration of empiric thrombolysis in an unstable patient. Tachycardia, hypoxia, and a plethoric IVC do not resolve the differential. A primary comparison found the sign in 70% of patients with PE and 67% with RV infarction. [Casazza et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15664548/).

**Correction:** Put the RV ischemia/infarction mimic beside the sign. Explain that emergency PE reperfusion without CT depends on severe instability, high clinical probability, convincing RV pressure overload, no better explanation, and inability to obtain CT safely. [ESC PE guideline, unstable-patient pathway](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/41/4/543/5556136).

**Counterexample:** The module's own inferior-STEMI/RV-infarction case supplies an alternative cause. This finding does **not** reject immediate reperfusion in the profoundly unstable, high-probability PE scenario in Case 1.

## Moderate-priority findings

### M1-01 — Moderate: Apical screen sides are reversed, and the preset explanation confuses depth with orientation

**Locations:** [machine setup](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/01-windows.qmd:26), [A4C table](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/01-windows.qmd:247), [A5C table](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/01-windows.qmd:277), [quiz answer](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/01-windows.qmd:327).

The module explicitly teaches a screen-right indicator and a probe marker toward the patient's left for apical imaging, but places LV/LA on screen left and RV/RA on screen right. Standard A4C under that convention has the opposite arrangement. The cardiac preset does not invert depth to bring the apex to the top; the apex is near the transducer. [ASE comprehensive TTE guideline, apical views](https://www.asecho.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/2019_Comprehensive-TTE.pdf).

**Correction:** Correct the A4C/A5C tables and quiz together, distinguish horizontal display convention from near/far field, and reconcile all associated diagrams and clips with the chosen convention.

**Counterexample:** A correctly acquired standard A4C would be mislabeled by the present answer key. This is a text inconsistency established without assuming every embedded clip uses the same display setting.

### M1-02 — Moderate: PLAX teaching misplaces the descending aorta and asks for structures outside the usual plane

**Locations:** [optimization](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/01-windows.qmd:115), [aortic-valve table entry](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/01-windows.qmd:132), [descending-aorta landmark](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/01-windows.qmd:134).

The descending aorta is described twice as far left; the useful anatomical landmark is posterior to the LA. The text also implies that a normal PLAX should show the true apex and three aortic leaflets. Standard PLAX usually excludes the true apex and shows two aortic cusps; three-cusp assessment belongs in PSAX. [ASE comprehensive TTE guideline, PLAX acquisition and figures](https://www.asecho.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/2019_Comprehensive-TTE.pdf).

**Correction:** Teach anatomy relative to the LA, maximize the true LV long axis without chasing an apparent apex, and distinguish PLAX from PSAX cusp appearances. Check the PLAX diagram during remediation.

**Counterexample:** A learner may abandon a correct PLAX to obtain a misleading oblique view, or search the wrong region for the aortic landmark used later to distinguish pleural from pericardial fluid.

### M1-03 — Moderate: The IVC introduction primes a fluid-responsiveness rule that Module 5 correctly rejects

**Location:** [IVC rationale](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/01-windows.qmd:101); compare [Module 5 overview](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/05-ivc.qmd:18).

Calling this view the basis for assessing volume status and fluid responsiveness presents diameter/respiratory variation too generally. Its role in estimating RA pressure does not make it a universal measure of circulating volume or response to a fluid bolus. Ventilation and other conditions limit interpretation. [ASE 2025 right-heart guideline](https://www.asecho.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/PIIS0894731725000379.pdf), [ESICM 2025 circulatory shock/hemodynamic monitoring guideline](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00134-025-08137-z).

**Correction:** Introduce IVC imaging as one contribution to right-sided pressure and congestion assessment, with context-dependent respiratory behavior; refer to Module 5 for dynamic assessment and limitations.

**Counterexample:** A large IVC after a ventilator change can coexist with fluid responsiveness. Module 5 already explains this; the introductory framing should agree.

### M2-04 — Moderate: Stable suspected NSTE-ACS becomes definitive NSTEMI and automatic immediate catheterization

**Location:** [Case 3 answer](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/02-lv.qmd:275), following the stable scenario at [line 262](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/02-lv.qmd:262).

Chest pain, ST depression, and lateral RWMA warrant urgent ACS evaluation. They do not alone establish acute myocardial infarction or supply the very-high-risk features used to choose immediate angiography. The stem does not specify refractory ischemia despite treatment, instability, or dangerous arrhythmia. [ESC 2023 ACS guideline, section 5.1 and Figure 8](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehad191).

**Correction:** Use suspected high-risk NSTE-ACS, serial ECGs/hs-troponin, appropriate initial care, urgent cardiology involvement, and risk-based invasive timing. If immediate cath is the desired answer, add the qualifying clinical feature. Troponin should not delay emergency care when that feature is present.

**Counterexample:** An old lateral RWMA with unchanged ST depression and resolved pain satisfies much of the supplied description without proving an acute culprit occlusion. The concern is automatic immediate activation, not urgent assessment.

### M2-05 — Moderate: Basal sparing is too decisive in the Takotsubo-versus-LAD distinction

**Locations:** [Takotsubo section](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/02-lv.qmd:208), [quiz answer](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/02-lv.qmd:337).

The explanation implies LAD occlusion must include basal anterior dysfunction. Distal LAD disease can spare basal myocardium. A pattern extending beyond one coronary territory raises suspicion for Takotsubo but does not independently settle the acute coronary differential. [International Takotsubo consensus, Part II](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5991205/).

**Correction:** Make basal sparing a clue, not a discriminator sufficient to exclude ACS; explain that clinical context and appropriate coronary evaluation are required.

**Counterexample:** Distal LAD occlusion with apical dysfunction and preserved basal contraction could be mislabeled as stress cardiomyopathy by the current rule.

### M2-06 — Moderate: An uncertain LV examination is explicitly converted into a normal result

**Location:** [normal LV description](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/02-lv.qmd:87).

The sentence “if you are unsure whether the function is normal, it probably is” teaches reassurance precisely when additional views or review are needed. Near-cavity obliteration is also used for both normal and hyperdynamic reference descriptions, blurring that distinction. ASE FoCUS recommendations explicitly recognize nondiagnostic examinations. [ASE FoCUS recommendations](https://www.asecho.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/01/FCU.pdf).

**Correction:** Remove the reassurance rule. Judge systolic thickening and cavity change across adequate views; document limited/uncertain assessment and seek better imaging or review when it affects care. Clarify the two visual descriptions.

**Counterexample:** Foreshortening or poor endocardial definition can conceal dysfunction and leave a novice uncertain. Uncertainty is not evidence of normality.

### M3-03 — Moderate: ARDS advice oversimplifies PEEP's RV effect and leaves normocapnia unbounded

**Location:** [Case 3 management](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/03-rv.qmd:214).

High PEEP does not invariably increase pulmonary vascular resistance; the balance of recruitment and overdistension matters. A primary ARDS study demonstrated different responses by recruitability. The instruction to target normocapnia lacks an explicit lung-protective limit, while proning is only a generic consideration despite P:F 90. [Primary PEEP/recruitability study](https://academic.oup.com/ajrccm/article/210/7/900/8491230), [ATS 2024 ARDS guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/10870893/).

**Correction:** Individualize PEEP using mechanics, oxygenation, and hemodynamic response; limit harmful hypercapnia/acidemia while preserving lung-protective ventilation; prioritize prolonged proning in severe ARDS absent contraindications. Describe inhaled vasodilators as selected rescue therapy.

**Counterexamples:** Lowering PEEP in a recruitable lung can cause derecruitment. Increasing tidal volume or pressure solely to normalize CO₂ can increase ventilator injury.

### M3-04 — Moderate: RV wall thickness becomes a binary clock for acute versus chronic disease

**Location:** [acute/chronic distinction](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/03-rv.qmd:108).

The equals-sign rules imply that lack of hypertrophy proves an acute process and hypertrophy identifies a purely chronic one. Wall thickness provides context, not a reliable date of onset or exclusion of superimposed acute disease. Acquisition also matters: measure at end diastole and exclude trabeculations. [ASE 2025 right-heart guideline](https://www.asecho.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/PIIS0894731725000379.pdf).

**Correction:** Replace the equations with “supports/suggests,” explicitly allow acute-on-chronic disease, and compare previous studies when available. Teach appropriate wall-thickness measurement.

**Counterexample:** A new PE can cause sudden shock in a patient whose established PH already produced RV hypertrophy.

### M3-05 — Moderate: The collapsed PE patient is assigned a FAST examination

**Location:** [Case 1 examination choice](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/03-rv.qmd:158).

“FAST exam” is the wrong name/scope for the RV-focused cardiac assessment interpreted in the answer. FAST's cardiac component addresses pericardial fluid; a negative trauma FAST does not answer the RV shock question. International FoCUS recommendations explicitly caution against extending this FAST terminology to focused cardiac evaluation. [International FoCUS recommendations](https://www.pocus.org/wp-content/uploads/pdf/Via-JASE-2014-International-FoCUS-Recommendations-2.pdf).

**Correction:** Specify immediate focused cardiac ultrasound as part of a shock examination, adding lung/proximal venous assessment when useful without delaying treatment.

**Counterexample:** A technically correct FAST can be negative while severe RV pressure overload from PE is present.

### M3-06 — Moderate: Peri-arrest alteplase dosing and catheter rescue need protocol qualification

**Location:** [Case 1 management](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/03-rv.qmd:170).

The answer presents 50 mg alteplase as a bolus for imminent arrest as though it were interchangeable standard dosing with 100 mg over two hours. Accelerated peri-arrest regimens vary; ERC 2025 finds insufficient evidence to prescribe an optimal fibrinolytic drug/dose during CPR. [ERC 2025 special-circumstances guideline](https://www.erc.edu/media/wwufbysp/gl2025-06-spec-circ-e.pdf).

**Correction:** Preserve urgent reperfusion but identify accelerated dosing as dependent on the institutional PE/arrest protocol. When fibrinolysis is contraindicated, distinguish mechanical catheter treatment or surgery from catheter-delivered thrombolysis. [2026 multisociety acute PE guideline](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.11.005).

**Counterexample:** Intracranial bleeding does not cease to be relevant because alteplase is delivered through a catheter. This finding is a missing protocol distinction, not a claim that a 50-mg bolus is never appropriate.

### M5-01 — Moderate: PLR is central to decisions but its performance and positive threshold are underspecified

**Locations:** [dynamic assessment](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/05-ivc.qmd:121), [quiz Q1 answer](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/05-ivc.qmd:191).

The module correctly calls for real-time stroke-volume/cardiac-output measurement and rejects blood pressure alone. However, “a reproducible increase” has no magnitude, timing, or measurement-precision qualification. A learner could count a small change within measurement noise as a positive test. This is a practical teaching gap rather than a reversal of the module's sound physiology.

**Correction:** Add a concise protocol: start semirecumbent, lower the trunk while raising the legs to approximately 45°, capture the transient peak response promptly with a consistent real-time method, and confirm return toward baseline. Specify a meaningful change using that method's validated threshold and precision; a roughly 10% CO/SV rise is commonly used, but borderline changes require caution. Do not turn it into a universal threshold for every acquisition method. [Monnet et al., original PLR study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16540963/), [Monnet and Teboul, practical PLR rules](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4293822/).

**Counterexample:** LVOT VTI changing from 15.0 to 15.5 cm is approximately 3.3%; reproducibility alone does not establish a clinically meaningful response. Preserve the existing distinction between responsiveness, need for fluid, and tolerance.

## Low-priority finding

### M4-01 — Low: Cancer-associated effusion is labeled malignant, and the quiz adds decisive facts

**Locations:** [Case 2 answer](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/04-pericardium.qmd:232), [quiz Q4 stem](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/04-pericardium.qmd:340), [answer](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/04-pericardium.qmd:344).

Cancer history makes malignant involvement plausible but does not establish effusion etiology; definitive neoplastic involvement requires cytology or tissue evidence. The quiz also introduces “very large,” “symptomatic,” and “malignant” in its answer although the stem gives a large effusion, metastatic cancer, and no definite collapse. [ESC 2025 myocarditis/pericarditis guideline, section 11.2](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/46/40/3952/8234483).

**Correction:** Use cancer-associated or suspected malignant effusion. Either refer explicitly to Case 2 or add its progressive dyspnea, tachycardia, and JVD to the question. Preserve the correct lesson that absent collapse alone does not establish safe discharge.

**Counterexamples:** Cancer therapy or infection may cause the effusion. A previously evaluated, asymptomatic stable effusion under a specialist outpatient plan is different from Case 2. This is not a recommendation to discharge an unevaluated patient.

## Findings deliberately not overstated

- **Module 3 Case 2:** Consideration of early catheter treatment is not categorically wrong. HI-PEITHO, published in March 2026, studied selected intermediate-risk patients with RV/LV ≥1, elevated troponin, and at least two cardiorespiratory distress criteria. The case already meets its BP and HR thresholds; its RV ratio and treatment-selection reasoning could be clearer. This is not evidence to treat every normotensive PE invasively. [HI-PEITHO primary trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41910345/).
- **Module 4:** The text usefully separates clinical tamponade from isolated echo findings, covers PPV/PH/low-pressure and loculated exceptions, and gives appropriately urgent drainage or surgical escalation in its unstable cases. Constrictive pericarditis is explicitly outside the module's agreed scope, so its absence is not a defect.
- **Module 5:** The current text separates RA pressure, congestion, responsiveness, and tolerance; explains the different IVC measurements/thresholds; acknowledges VExUS's original validation setting; and handles TR, missing Doppler windows, ventilation, and mixed shock. Preserve these qualifications. A brief future note on rhythm effects on hepatic waveforms would be useful but is not another prioritized finding.

## Review coverage and limits

All five source modules, their cases and quizzes, relevant local guidance, and attribution records were reviewed. Independent reviews covered acquisition/LV, RV, and pericardium; the consolidating review covered IVC/VExUS and cross-module consistency. Clinical claims were checked against professional guidelines and primary studies available on the review date. Definitive errors, missing qualifications, and instructional gaps are identified separately in the explanations above.

All **95 local image references** in these five modules resolve to existing files: M1 22, M2 25, M3 21, M4 16, M5 11. These are references, including repeated assets and diagrams, not 95 unique cine loops. Existence does not verify diagnostic content. Full moving GIF/AVIF sequences and external lecture videos were not comprehensively adjudicated. Uncertain provenance or a suggestive filename was not treated as proof of a scenario mismatch. This is a text/source review, not a clinical certification or a complete visual-media audit.

Teaching QMD files and generated site pages were not changed. Only this report and the roadmap were added/updated for this review.
