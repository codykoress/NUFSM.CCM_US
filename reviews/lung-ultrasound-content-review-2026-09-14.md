# Lung ultrasound module: adversarial content review

Reviewed 2026-09-14. Scope: `modules/08-lung.qmd`, all four integration cases and five quiz answers, corresponding passages in the checked-in HTML, media references, credits, and curriculum mapping. This is display Module 06 despite its filename and current page title.

**Assessment:** Revise before using this as a standalone guide to clinical decisions. The recurring problem is that a useful ultrasound finding becomes a definitive diagnosis, exclusion, or treatment instruction without enough supporting information. Five findings below warrant priority correction. The remaining findings affect diagnostic fidelity, acquisition, or teaching consistency.

The proposed corrections are review recommendations; the module has not been edited. Clinical claims were checked against original studies and professional guidance. All nine referenced GIF files exist. Case 4 was inspected through sampled frames and repository documentation; this was not a full expert adjudication of all cine loops. External lecture content was not audited.

**1. High priority — A missing lung point incorrectly removes pneumothorax from the pathway.**

Locations: [negative lung-point branch](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:260), [deteriorating post-intubation patient](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:310), and [the module's own decompression instruction](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:182).

The BLUE table sends a patient without a lung point to COPD, pleurodesis, or ARDS. Case 2 then asks learners to find a lung point in a deteriorating patient. A large pneumothorax can have no accessible transition to apposed lung. The original lung-point study found the sign in only 44 of 66 pneumothoraces. This is a rule-in sign, not a required finding. [Original lung-point study](https://www.ultrasoundleadershipacademy.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/01/Intensive_Care_Med_2000_Lichtenstein.pdf).

**Proposed correction:** Make the negative branch “pneumothorax remains possible; integrate other signs, physiology, and further imaging when stable.” In Case 2, assess tube position and ventilation promptly, but do not make either a fixed 2-cm tube withdrawal or a lung-point search a prerequisite to treating clinically suspected tension physiology. The current case contradicts the earlier instruction to avoid an imaging delay.

**Adversarial check:** A patient develops severe hypoxemia and shock after positive-pressure ventilation, has absent sliding/B-lines/pulse, and has no lung point. The lesson must not direct the learner away from pneumothorax or delay emergency treatment. [WSES-AAST thoracic trauma guidance](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13017-025-00651-1).

**2. High priority — Case 1 uses a negative consolidation scan to withhold antibiotics.**

Locations: [B-line treatment rule](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:117) and [Case 1 answer](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:291).

Hypertension, known HFrEF, and homogeneous B-lines make cardiogenic edema a strong working diagnosis. They do not justify the separate assertion that antibiotics should be withheld because no consolidation is visible. Pneumonia can be missed by lung ultrasound, including when lesions do not reach an accessible pleural surface. A prospective multicenter study explicitly found that a negative examination does not exclude pneumonia. [Reissig et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22700780/).

**Proposed correction:** Preserve treatment of the likely hypertensive pulmonary edema. Base an antibiotic decision on the overall likelihood of infection, with reassessment and further evaluation when indicated. Replace “clearing confirms cardiogenic etiology” with a statement that improvement supports successful decongestion; it does not establish a single cause or exclude concurrent infection.

**Adversarial check:** A patient with chronic reduced EF has both congestion and pneumonia. Improvement in congestion must not prematurely close the infection assessment.

**3. High priority — Static bronchograms become a bronchoscopy-versus-antibiotics decision rule.**

Locations: [consolidation teaching point](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:216), [Case 4 answer](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:348), and [quiz Q4](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:384).

Case 4 recommends bronchoscopy for static bronchograms and introduces antibiotics only in the hypothetical dynamic-bronchogram alternative. Yet 20 of 52 patients with proven pneumonia had static bronchograms in the original study. Dynamic bronchograms were relatively specific for pneumonia versus resorptive atelectasis, but sensitivity was only 61%. The converse inference is invalid. [Lichtenstein et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19225063/).

**Proposed correction:** Static bronchograms leave pneumonia and atelectasis in the differential. Evaluate obstruction using secretion burden, volume loss, tube position, and other evidence. Reserve bronchoscopy for a supported indication. In this febrile, increasingly hypoxemic ventilated patient, assess and treat suspected pneumonia on clinical grounds while investigating collapse. Routine diagnostic BAL is not required for every suspected VAP. [ATS/IDSA HAP/VAP guidance](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/hap_vap/).

**Adversarial check:** Proven pneumonia with static bronchograms must not be routed to bronchoscopy alone or have treatment deferred until dynamic bronchograms appear.

**4. High priority — Complex fluid is treated as an automatic chest-tube indication.**

Locations: [effusion teaching point](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:152) and [consolidation plus effusion rule](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:217).

Echogenicity or septation alone does not establish pleural infection or mandate a tube. The module also treats consolidation beside an effusion as presumptively parapneumonic, although the effusion itself can compress lung. The result is a route from a nonspecific image directly to an invasive procedure.

**Proposed correction:** Distinguish fluid detection, characterization, diagnostic sampling, and therapeutic drainage. For suspected pleural infection, integrate pus, microbiology, pleural-fluid pH and other chemistry, symptoms, accessible volume, and imaging. BTS recommends drainage for appropriate high-risk infection, including pH ≤7.2 when access is safe; septation contributes to an intermediate-risk decision rather than replacing the assessment. [BTS pleural disease guideline, 2023](https://www.brit-thoracic.org.uk/document-library/guidelines/pleural-disease/pleural-disease-full-supplement/).

**Adversarial check:** A complex malignant or chronic inflammatory effusion should not trigger a chest tube solely because it is complex. Anechoic fluid must not be assumed uninfected.

**5. High priority — The effusion-volume equation is wrong by a factor of ten.**

Location: [volume estimation](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:146).

The module multiplies depth in centimeters by 20. The Balik equation uses interpleural separation in millimeters: volume in mL ≈ 20 × separation in mm, equivalent to 200 × separation in cm. Thus 5 cm yields approximately 1,000 mL under that model, rather than the module's 100 mL. Calling the formula approximate does not correct the unit error. [Balik et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16432674/).

**Proposed correction:** Correct the units and specify the measurement technique and study population, or remove numeric estimation from the core lesson. This was an estimate developed in selected mechanically ventilated patients, not a universal conversion of any observed fluid depth. Estimated volume also does not establish a safe insertion site.

**6. Moderate priority — The BLUE section changes the algorithm and mislabels its performance.**

Locations: [anterior branches](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:237), [DVT branch](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:245), [PLAPS branch](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:252), and [quiz Q5](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:391).

The original B-profile requires sliding. The module drops that condition, omits the B′ and C pneumonia profiles, and leaves overlapping “B-lines” and “absent sliding” routes. It also labels 97% for edema and 81% for PE as PPVs; these are sensitivities. [Original BLUE study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3734893/).

The reported cohort PPVs were approximately 87% for the B-profile and 94% for A-profile plus DVT. These are population-dependent results, not fixed probabilities for every patient. [Lichtenstein's BLUE/FALLS account, Table 2](https://journal.chestnet.org/article/S0012-3692%2815%2937223-8/fulltext).

**Proposed correction:** Restore the complete profile definitions, label metrics correctly, and describe BLUE as a framework that suggests a diagnosis. A negative DVT scan does not exclude PE; an A-profile does not categorically exclude pulmonary edema. The original study itself included edema with anterior A-lines and PE without thrombosis, and its 90.5% result followed exclusion of uncertain, mixed, and rare diagnoses. [Original study and limitations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3734893/).

**Adversarial check:** Bilateral B-lines without sliding should not enter two contradictory branches. Persistent PE suspicion should survive a negative compression examination.

**7. Moderate priority — The pleural-line depth is referenced to the wrong structure.**

Location: [scanning technique](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:52).

The text locates the pleural line approximately 0.5 cm below the skin. The familiar teaching landmark is approximately 0.5 cm deeper than the rib line in adults. Skin-to-pleura depth varies with chest-wall thickness. [Lichtenstein, Figure 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24401163/).

**Proposed correction:** Teach identification of ribs, rib shadows, and the pleural interface before interpreting motion or artifacts. A fixed skin-depth rule could lead a novice to interpret superficial fascia as pleura, particularly in obesity.

**8. Moderate priority — B-line grading conflates a positive window with an etiology.**

Locations: [opening B-line claim](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:15), [grading and distribution](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:100), and [quiz Q1](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:363).

The lesson reduces diffuse B-lines to edema and labels confluent artifacts severe alveolar edema. Fibrosis and other causes of reduced aeration deserve explicit treatment. A positive window is also not the same as diffuse bilateral disease. The text alternates between “zone” and “intercostal space” without defining a counting protocol.

**Proposed correction:** Define the scanning/counting method, then describe burden, distribution, pleural morphology, associated consolidation, and clinical context. In the EACVI acute-HF approach, a typical pattern includes at least three B-lines in a zone and at least two positive zones on each side. Sparse dependent artifacts may occur in healthy lungs. Neither a count nor preserved/reduced EF independently determines the cause. [EACVI consensus, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11032195/).

**Adversarial check:** Chronic basal fibrosis must not automatically produce an acute-edema diagnosis and diuresis recommendation.

**9. Moderate priority — Case 3 uses lung-point position as a sufficient indication for a chest tube.**

Location: [trauma case management](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:329).

A midaxillary lung point is converted into a “moderate” pneumothorax and then an unconditional tube recommendation. Position can correlate with extent, but it is not a complete measurement or management assessment. Chest drainage may be appropriate in this case; the concern is that the stated evidence does not establish the indication.

**Proposed correction:** Specify symptoms, respiratory support, measured extent, progression, associated injury, and capacity for observation. Contemporary trauma guidance allows observation of selected small pneumothoraces in stable patients and emphasizes respiratory/hemodynamic impairment and ventilation in drainage decisions. Do not prescribe observation for this patient without that missing information either. [WSES-AAST guidance](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13017-025-00651-1).

**Adversarial check:** A learner must be able to explain the indication for drainage beyond the position of a single sonographic sign.

**10. Moderate priority — Case 4 has a documented mismatch between the clip and the decisive finding.**

Locations: [embedded clip](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:341), [static-bronchogram description](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:345), and [repository media notes](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/CLAUDE.md).

The embedded image is therefore not a validated illustration of the feature on which the answer depends. Sampled frames do not substitute for formal cine interpretation.

**Proposed correction:** Have a clinician adjudicate the complete loop and either align the case with its actual findings or replace it with a verified matching example. If neither is immediately feasible, use an explicit text placeholder as the project's media policy allows. After correction, ask learners to describe bronchogram motion relative to the surrounding consolidated tissue, rather than simply any movement with respiration.

**11. Moderate priority — Acquisition and reassessment are not reproducible enough for the decisions taught.**

Locations: [BLUE landmark descriptions](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:42), [PLAPS description](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:46), [serial B-lines](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:116), and [quiz Q3](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:377).

“Upper/lower third” and “continued posteriorly” leave the exact windows ambiguous. The anatomy callout is text rather than a diagram. No lung-specific settings, documentation template, or reproducible serial-exam method is supplied. A single dependent point cannot establish absence of consolidation throughout the lung; consolidation is not material that necessarily “accumulates” there.

**Proposed correction:** Supply explicit anatomical landmarks, a coverage diagram, and guidance to expand beyond rapid BLUE points when the question requires it. Teach diaphragm/abdominal-organ identification for basal scans. Record zones obtained or inaccessible, position, respiratory support, probe/settings, and findings. Use consistent acquisition for trends; recognize that positioning and ventilation can affect appearance. Current international guidance recommends broad accessible chest coverage and standardized acquisition, including avoiding artifact-altering filters/compounding for artifact assessment. [International LUS consensus, 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jum.16088).

**Adversarial check:** Two learners should obtain comparable studies, and an incomplete examination must not be recorded as a negative examination.

**12. Lower priority — Navigation, references, and quiz design reinforce the content problems.**

Locations: [page title](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:2), [Module 11 referral](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:153), [cardiac integration referral](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:266), and [quiz](/C:/Users/codyk/GitHub/NUFSM.CCM_US/modules/08-lung.qmd:356).

The procedural Module 11 was removed, and the cardiac-echo reference to Module 6 conflicts with the current display mapping. The module has no clinical References section. The quiz repeats the disputed PPV and airway-patency claims, and it predominantly tests recall of the module's rules.

**Proposed correction:** Correct displayed numbering and use actual links for cross-references without renaming source files. Add source citations beside consequential claims and a short reference list. Replace or extend quiz items with the counterexamples above: absent lung point, static bronchograms with suspected infection, complex fluid without an established drainage indication, negative DVT with persistent PE suspicion, and an incomplete scan.

**Revision approach:** Address findings 1–5 first, then rebuild the BLUE section and synchronize its cases and quiz. Correct acquisition and media before treating the revised lesson as ready for independent use. Retain the useful distinction that sliding, a true B-line, or lung pulse excludes pneumothorax only at the examined location. Add an explicit step between observing a sign and deciding treatment: what remains uncertain, what additional evidence is needed, and what clinical change would trigger escalation.
